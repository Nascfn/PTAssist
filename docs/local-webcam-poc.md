# Local webcam research prototype

This local experiment is separate from the team's upload-only Fall 2026 web-app proof of concept. It uses a laptop webcam to explore pose tracking and real-time feedback.

## Local exercise analysis proof of concept

This Python desktop POC analyzes one person doing side-view squats, side-view stepping lunges, a front-view one-arm cross-body stretch, a standing toe touch, a shoulder side raise, or a one-arm elbow bend with a stationary laptop webcam. It also has a custom-exercise mode that learns a local *movement template* from approved examples; it does not train a pose model. Pose estimation and analysis run entirely on the laptop. No account, backend, database, cloud API, or LLM is used at runtime.

### Run on Windows

From the repository root in PowerShell:

```powershell
python -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -r requirements.txt
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m ptassist_squat.app
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m ptassist_squat.app --exercise lunge
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m ptassist_squat.app --exercise cross-body-stretch
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m ptassist_squat.app --exercise toe-touch
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m ptassist_squat.app --exercise shoulder-abduction
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m ptassist_squat.app --exercise elbow-bend
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m ptassist_squat.custom_app --name side-arm-raise
```

In custom mode, keep both shoulders, both hips, and the moving joints visible, and use the same camera position for teaching and later attempts. For a left-arm side raise, a **front-facing waist-up view** is enough if the elbow and wrist stay visible; knees and feet are not required. The camera preview is mirrored like a selfie view; `--no-mirror` restores the original orientation without changing pose analysis. Press **Space** once near the camera to start a teaching batch, walk back, and pause in the starting pose until the display says ready. Do two or more complete raises, returning to the starting pose and pausing between them. Walk forward and press **Space** again to stop. The app excludes the walk by waiting for a stable pose and checking that the hips stay in the exercise spot during each candidate rep. Press **V** to watch each candidate's mirrored webcam video with its detected skeleton; **A** approves only after opening that review, and **X** discards it. Space pauses replay, Left/Right step frames, R restarts it, and Q returns to approval. Approved examples and their MP4s are saved locally in `references/` and `sessions/`. Approve at least two consistent examples, then press **L** to build a local reference in `references/custom_<name>.json`.

Once a reference exists, choose **Learn exercise** or **Do exercise** with the on-screen buttons (or L/D). Learn exercise shows your approved demonstration's farthest wrist position as a cyan stick person and draws an arrow from the tracked wrist toward it. It is a guide to *your demonstration*, not a prescribed safe range. Do exercise counts complete attempts and compares their path with the examples, showing an orange correction arrow for the most divergent joint. Both modes wait for a stable starting pose at the taught camera position; walking toward the computer, moving the camera, and long tracking gaps cancel an attempt. Press **V** to replay the latest completed attempt; Space pauses, Left/Right seeks, and Q closes the replay. Click **Menu** or press **M** to switch modes, **T** to teach a fresh set, and **F** to toggle fullscreen. The window is resizable. A later run with the same name loads the saved template. Example: `--name side-arm-raise` loads `references/custom_side-arm-raise.json`.

For a file-based check without the chooser, add `--video C:\path\to\clip.mp4 --mode learn --headless` or use `--mode do`. File clips need a stationary starting pose before the rep to let the detector arm; a clip cut exactly at movement onset may display the pose but count zero attempts.

Custom teaching is intentionally user-labeled: approving a demonstration means you want to compare against it, **not** that the movement is safe or clinically correct. The teaching capture currently targets stationary, in-place movements like an arm raise; a step or lunge should use its dedicated mode. It expects a movement that returns near its starting pose and uses visible shoulders and hips to normalize camera distance. It cannot reliably learn movement from joints that are obscured, or distinguish a good exercise from consistently bad demonstrations. Keep exercise names simple; each name has its own reference.

A front-facing one-arm side raise is a useful *technical* test because the shoulder, elbow, and wrist have a visible sideways path. [Worcestershire Acute Hospitals NHS Trust describes shoulder abduction/adduction](https://www.worcsacute.nhs.uk/leaflets/upper-limb-exercise/) among upper-limb exercises, and explicitly says people should perform only exercises their therapist has instructed. This POC's arrow reproduces a user-approved example; it does not select an appropriate range of motion or give treatment advice. A side view can hide a sideways raise, while an angled view can make a forward raise appear similar. Keep the camera stationary and near chest height, face it, and leave room for the whole moving arm. If the view or camera distance changes, teach a new reference. A single camera cannot verify the arm's depth, subtle shoulder rotation, or pain.

For squats, place the camera so your head, shoulders, hips, knees, ankles, heels and toes fit in the image throughout the movement and stand sideways. For lunges, show both legs side-on, start with feet together, step one leg forward, bend, rise, and step back. For the cross-body stretch, face the camera and keep both shoulders plus the stretching wrist visible; you may sit or stand. Start with an arm lowered, bring it across the chest near shoulder height, hold for at least 1.5 seconds, then lower it to count one movement cycle. For the standing toe touch, stand sideways with your reaching hand and feet visible, bend forward, then return upright. Keep the camera fixed and use even lighting. The display starts in a resizable window; drag its edges or corners to change its size. The video stays centered without stretching. Press **F** to toggle fullscreen, or **Q** or **Esc** to end and save a JSON session in `sessions/`. Use `--fullscreen` to start fullscreen.

For the **shoulder-abduction example**, face the camera with both shoulders, both hips, and one moving arm visible. Start with that arm at your side, lift it sideways in an arc toward overhead, then lower it back to your side. The video is mirrored like a selfie view. A fixed start/overhead stick-person example and brief instructions sit beside the video. A short orange arrow beside the arm shows the *angular direction* of the current phase; it disappears at the target angle and at rest. There is no cyan trajectory, blue line, or fixed wrist-position target. The sidebar displays 2D shoulder, elbow, and torso angles. A side raise and return counts as a movement **cycle**; `full_arc_reached` records whether the upper-arm shoulder angle reached the experimental **165° near-overhead cutoff**. A short cycle is recorded but marked incomplete. Noticeable elbow bend and torso lean are flagged separately. These image-plane measurements cannot establish true 3D shoulder abduction, distinguish a sideways raise from a forward raise in depth, assess scapular motion, or determine a safe range or rotator-cuff health. The cutoff is an engineering value, **not a clinical target**; use only the range prescribed by your therapist. [Cambridge University Hospitals describes shoulder abduction as lifting the arm sideways](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/shoulder-range-of-movement-exercises-post-surgery/). The settings are in [`shoulder_abduction_config.json`](../shoulder_abduction_config.json).

For the simpler **elbow-bend example**, show the camera your upper body and one unobstructed arm. Stand or sit still, let that arm hang straight at your side until the app says to bend, bend your elbow to bring the hand toward your shoulder, then straighten it again. The camera image is mirrored; the sidebar shows a two-pose stick figure, current elbow angle, and the current instruction. A curved orange arrow centered on the elbow shows bend or straighten direction; when the whole upper arm rises, an arrow at the elbow points it back down. A bend-and-return counts as a cycle. The experimental 95° cutoff distinguishes a full bend from a short bend; it is **not a prescribed target**. The upper-arm angle also flags raising the whole arm instead of bending mostly at the elbow. The mode needs only shoulder, elbow, and wrist landmarks for one arm, so the legs can be outside the frame. If the elbow or wrist is hidden, it abstains. [Leeds Teaching Hospitals describes an elbow bend-to-straighten movement](https://www.leedsth.nhs.uk/patients/resources/elbow-exercises/); exercise range should follow your therapist's instructions, not this demo's cutoff. Settings are in [`elbow_bend_config.json`](../elbow_bend_config.json).

For squat and lunge reference feedback, complete a rep and read its displayed knee, torso, stance, and duration measurements. Press **A** to approve that latest rep as *your local reference*. The app writes `references/squat.json` or `references/lunge_left.json` / `references/lunge_right.json`; future sessions load them automatically. On later attempts, the app shows simple reference cues at the bottom of the movement and, after each completed rep, says whether measured knee depth, torso lean, duration, and (for lunges) step width are within configured tolerances. Reference cues pause whenever pose tracking is unreliable. Pressing **A** again replaces the matching reference with the latest rep. Left- and right-leading lunges use separate references. A user-approved example is a comparison target, **not** an independent clinical judgment of good form. If you move the camera or change view, approve a new reference. `references/` stays local and is Git-ignored; `--reference-dir` chooses another folder.

Completed attempts now save local annotated MP4 review clips and companion JSON timelines in `sessions/`. The live display marks a relevant knee or torso when an existing exercise's measured feedback flags it; custom mode instead marks joints that diverge from its demonstrated path. Press **V** in either app to replay the latest completed attempt, or run `python -m ptassist_squat.review sessions\your_attempt.mp4` later. These videos contain webcam images and are stored only on this machine; `sessions/` is Git-ignored. Pass `--no-review-clips` to the original exercise app to disable video saving. The timeline includes timestamps, feedback, confidence, and localized measurement or joint deviations. On low-confidence frames the app abstains and draws no form marker.

If camera 0 is not your laptop camera, use `--camera 1`. For a local video file, use `--video C:\path\to\squat.mp4`. That mode uses the video's frame rate for timestamps. `--output C:\path\to\session.json` chooses the session file. `--log-every 15` prints sampled measurements to the terminal. For detailed debugging, use `--trace sessions\my_check.jsonl`; this saves **every frame** with the model's landmark coordinates/confidences, selected-side raw angles, the smoothed angles used for decisions, state, foot separation, and specific missing/low-confidence/out-of-frame landmarks. Raw angles from rejected frames are marked diagnostic-only: they can be wrong when pose tracking fails. The trace is local pose data, not webcam video. `--headless --max-frames 60` runs a bounded camera check without a window. A separate first-stage camera/skeleton probe is available with `python -m ptassist_squat.webcam`.

The window shows the skeleton, exercise measurements, selected side, weakest required landmark confidence, state, rep or movement-cycle count, frame rate, and current feedback. A green border means the required landmarks are tracked and the currently checked movement cues pass; it does not certify overall form. A flashing orange border and feedback indicate a checked cue warning, such as squat torso lean above the configured limit, insufficient squat depth at bottom, stretching with the wrist far below shoulder height, or noticeable knee bend during a toe touch. Red means the pose cannot be assessed reliably or required landmarks are out of view; red is an abstention, not a form judgment. Arm straightness is shown as unassessed because overlapping arms distort elbow landmarks. `--preview C:\path\to\frame.png` writes the final annotated frame. The JSON has per-cycle metrics and minimum landmark reliability; `count_definition` specifies what was counted.

### How it works

`pose.py` uses the local MediaPipe Pose Landmarker Lite task model in VIDEO mode, with one person and timestamped OpenCV frames. It converts the results into generic normalized `Landmark` records. `geometry.py` computes 2D angles in pixel coordinates so camera aspect ratio does not distort them. `analysis.py` selects the visible body side, checks confidence and framing, applies a time-based exponential angle smoother, and advances through `STANDING → DESCENDING → BOTTOM → ASCENDING → STANDING`. A rep counts only at the final transition. A shallow but otherwise complete rep is counted and marked `minimum_depth_reached: false`; a tiny dip that never reaches BOTTOM is not counted.

`arm_stretch.py` consumes the same generic landmarks but has its own front-view checks and `READY → REACHING → HOLDING → RETURNING → READY` state machine. Cross-body reach is the wrist's horizontal progress from its own shoulder toward the opposite shoulder, scaled by shoulder width. The wrist must reach 75% of that span and stay near shoulder height for a continuous 1.5-second hold. One movement cycle counts after the wrist returns within 30% of its starting shoulder line. A short hold does not count. The elbow is drawn but not used for judging form: the live test showed that overlapping arms can make its estimated angle misleading even at high model confidence. Its thresholds are in [`arm_stretch_config.json`](../arm_stretch_config.json).

`shoulder_abduction.py` measures the moving upper arm relative to the torso's vertical axis, the elbow angle, and torso lean from a front view. It waits for a stable arm-down start and moves through `READY → RAISING → TOP → LOWERING → READY` for an overhead cycle; a shorter raise goes from `RAISING` to `LOWERING` and is marked as a short cycle. The upper-arm angle alone determines whether the overhead target was reached; wrist and outward-position measurements are diagnostic only. Movement away from the exercise spot or lost landmarks cancels an incomplete attempt. The orange cue uses the measured angle to point along the raise or return arc, with a separate elbow-extension cue if that angle indicates bend. All thresholds are experimental and should be tuned with clinician-reviewed recordings before use as health feedback.

`elbow_bend.py` uses a single shoulder-elbow-wrist angle and a secondary upper-arm-to-vertical angle. Its explicit `STRAIGHT → BENDING → BENT → STRAIGHTENING → STRAIGHT` cycle records shallow movements separately and flags upper-arm lifting. It locks the selected arm during a cycle and cancels long tracking gaps or large changes in camera distance/shoulder position. In the first guided live run, it assessed 811/868 frames (93.4%) and counted eight movements; the user reported doing about eight. Seven crossed the experimental bend cutoff. The first was a 0.61-second shallow movement, and three cycles carried an upper-arm-motion flag. These are measurement observations, not clinical form judgments.

`toe_touch.py` uses the same generic landmarks to measure fingertip-to-toe **image-plane distance**, scaled by the selected leg's hip-to-knee plus knee-to-ankle length. It tracks knee angle and torso lean from the side. Its `STANDING → BENDING → BOTTOM → RETURNING → STANDING` state machine counts a bend-and-return after the torso passes 50°; the person need not actually reach the toes. Each rep records closest distance, maximum knee flexion from the standing baseline, a noticeable-bend flag at 35°, and whether the fingertip came within 0.15 leg lengths of the toe **in the image**. Camera overlap cannot prove physical contact or flexibility. Thresholds are in [`toe_touch_config.json`](../toe_touch_config.json). All exercise analyzers are separate; the app selects one with `--exercise` while webcam capture, pose estimation, drawing, color feedback, and JSON output are shared.

`lunge.py` tracks both leg angles and ankle separation. Its `STANDING → STEPPING → BOTTOM → RISING → STANDING` state machine requires an asymmetric step away from the initial stance, lead-knee bend, rise, and a return to narrow standing. A narrow squat-like movement does not enter the lunge state machine. The moving leg becomes the lead side. `reference.py` stores only the user-approved rep metrics and compares later completed reps against them; it does not train a model. The current lunge detector does not rigorously distinguish forward from reverse step direction. Its settings are in [`lunge_config.json`](../lunge_config.json).

`custom.py` consumes the same provider-independent landmarks. It recenters each frame on the hip midpoint, scales by torso length, finds reliably tracked joints that actually move in all approved examples, and builds a median 32-step movement path. Example-to-example variation sets a joint-specific tolerance with a conservative floor. Later attempts start after a stable starting hold and clear excursion, end on return to the start pose, and use dynamic time warping to compare at different speeds. The recorded hip center and body scale guard against walk-up movements with the stationary camera. The real-time cyan guide uses the demonstrated apex pose in Learn exercise and the closest forward template phase in Do exercise. Missing active landmarks pause assessment and eventually cancel the attempt. `custom_app.py` owns the teaching controls and dashboard; `teaching_video.py` stores compact teaching clips; `review.py` writes and replays local attempt video. The thresholds are in [`custom_config.json`](../custom_config.json). This is example matching, not a trained general exercise classifier or a clinical form score.

The analyzer abstains with **“Unable to reliably assess”** when a required landmark is too uncertain. When confident landmarks clearly extend outside the frame, it asks the person to move back. It will not advance a squat while pose data is invalid and cancels an interrupted attempt after a prolonged gap. It also asks for a side view when reliable left/right shoulder or hip separation indicates a frontal pose. These checks reduce false feedback, but a single 2D camera cannot guarantee perfect view classification.

Because a lunge can bend one knee through the same angles as a squat, squat mode also checks both feet. It uses ankle separation when the opposite ankle is sufficiently confident; if that ankle is uncertain, it requires **both** opposite heel and toe landmarks as a fallback. It rejects a front-to-back separation greater than 0.6 times the selected side's shoulder-to-hip length and abstains if neither source is reliable. The live display shows the `Feet` ratio, which source supplied it, body-pose confidence, and separate foot-guard confidence. This is a conservative image-plane guard, not an exercise classifier; its cutoffs need more testing across camera angles and stances.

All engineering thresholds are in the exercise config files (`config.json`, `lunge_config.json`, `arm_stretch_config.json`, `toe_touch_config.json`, `shoulder_abduction_config.json`, `elbow_bend_config.json`, and `custom_config.json`). The initial values are **not clinically validated**. In particular, the confidence cutoffs, squat knee and lean cutoffs, squat ankle-separation limit (0.6 torso lengths), lunge step/phase cutoffs, reference-comparison tolerances, arm reach target (75% of shoulder span), hold time (1.5 seconds), toe-touch bend and proximity cutoffs, shoulder-abduction near-overhead/elbow/torso cutoffs, elbow-bend target and upper-arm cutoffs, custom motion/tolerance cutoffs, smoothing interval (0.12 s), and framing/view limits need testing with real users and camera placements. All measurements are image-plane approximations; they are sensitive to camera angle, perspective, clothing, and occlusion.

### Validation

On this machine with Python 3.13.14, MediaPipe 1.0.1, OpenCV 5.0.0.93, NumPy 2.5.2, and pytest 9.1.1, the laptop webcam opened and the pose estimator returned landmarks for 30/30 frames in the first camera test. The OpenCV display loop ran without error. During a live standing check, the selected side reached 0.82–0.88 landmark confidence and the upright knee measured roughly 174–179°. One later live squat completed the full state sequence and counted once, with a 2.206-second duration, a minimum knee angle near 71°, and minimum landmark reliability of 0.819. The depth flag was true; the lean flag was false, close to its 35° cutoff. Only 132 of 349 frames in that session were assessable because the user was out of the required full-body view for much of it. Shallow depth, exaggerated lean, and low-confidence handling were verified with synthetic normalized landmark sequences using `python -m pytest -q`; shallow and exaggerated-lean movements still need live validation.

A live front-view cross-body stretch run counted six reach–hold–return cycles with high wrist and shoulder confidence, matching the user's approximate six attempted cycles. The user judged only two as good form. Its first version gave a false "straighten your arm" warning because overlapping arms distorted the model's elbow estimate. The revised analyzer excludes elbow angle from the decision and counts movement cycles only; arm straightness and overall form are explicitly **not assessed**.

In a second live squat session, the user did squat, squat, lunge, moved the computer, then squat and lunge. The app counted **one of three squats** and **neither lunge**. The two early squat attempts entered the bend state but lost valid full-body tracking near the bottom for longer than the 0.75-second gap limit, so they were discarded. During the lunges, the ankle-separation check rejected frames with `Feet` above 0.6; the later squat completed the full state sequence. The counted rep had a 76.55° minimum knee angle, depth reached, and 38.69° maximum torso lean, triggering the engineering lean warning. This is a partial live validation: lunge rejection worked in this sequence, while squat recall remains poor when feet leave the frame or tracking drops. See `sessions/live_squat_lunge_guard.json` and its `.stdout.log` for the trace.

A third live check used `--trace sessions/traced_squat.jsonl`. The user moved the camera, then did one squat near the end; the app counted exactly one rep. Its knee angle went from about 179° standing to 78.38° at the bottom, then back above 165°; the rep lasted 2.0 seconds and exceeded the 35° torso-lean cutoff at 38.08°. Of 906 frames, 147 passed the squat assessment checks. During camera adjustment, ankle and foot landmarks were the most frequent rejection causes. Replaying all 906 saved landmark frames through `SquatAnalyzer` reproduced the saved state, count, feedback, and validity on every frame. The trace stores pose coordinates and measurements, not video frames.

After reviewing that trace, the squat torso-lean cutoff was raised from 35° to **45°** as an experimental, more permissive value. The earlier session JSON retains its original 35° config and flag; a 38.08° peak would not trigger the new cutoff. Live lean feedback now clears when the current angle returns below the cutoff, while the per-rep record still retains the maximum angle and whether it crossed the cutoff.

For live toe-touch validation, the user performed one near-toe reach, one deliberately short reach, and one reach with bent knees. All **three** bend-and-return cycles counted. Their closest projected fingertip-to-toe distances were **0.117**, **0.457**, and **0.121** leg lengths. Maximum knee flexion from the standing baseline was **26.8°**, **12.1°**, and **51.9°**. The original 20° bend-warning cutoff incorrectly flagged the first rep. Replaying all 714 saved landmark frames with a **35°** cutoff preserved the three counts and flagged only the deliberate bent-knee rep. The original session remains unchanged; `sessions/live_toe_touch_reanalyzed_35.json` records the tuned result. This cutoff is fitted to a small demonstration and needs testing across people and camera placements.

Squat reference approval, persistence, live bottom-phase cues, comparison, and lunge movement phases have automated tests. Both modes ran through bounded webcam smoke tests without application errors; those short tests did not include full exercise movements. A saved real squat landmark trace replayed as a squat counted one rep and, when replayed through the lunge analyzer, counted zero lunges. For cross-session reference validation, one recorded squat was approved in a separate local validation folder; two other real squat session summaries fell within its metric tolerances, while a synthetic shallower variation produced a specific knee-depth difference.

In a live reference check, the user attempted a couple of squats before one counted; they pressed **A**, which saved `references/squat.json`, then ended the session without a later comparison rep. The original run assessed 233/1809 frames and counted one squat. Its first attempts lost the opposite ankle near the bottom while the heel and toe stayed trackable. Replaying the saved landmarks with the new corroborating-foot fallback counts **three** complete squat cycles, consistent with the user's report, and keeps narrow squats distinct from the wide-stance lunge guard in synthetic tests. The reanalysis is in `sessions/live_squat_reference_reanalyzed_foot_fallback.json`; the original user-approved reference and session remain unchanged. Live post-approval comparison and live lunge counting still need validation.

For this extension, automated tests cover learning from two complete examples, saving/loading templates, automatic attempt detection, an intentionally deviating wrist, low-confidence abstention, joint-guide rendering, review-video writing, and the resizable mode buttons. Using the saved real squat landmark trace, two recorded movement cycles formed a custom template; the automatic detector found one later cycle and scored it **88.2% similar** (a manually bounded comparison scored 90.5%). The reproducible result is in `sessions/custom_saved_trace_validation.json`. In a subsequent live left-arm-raise teaching session, five approved candidate traces each contained a clear left-wrist excursion of 1.26–1.45 torso lengths and very little right-arm or hip movement. The app learned the left elbow and wrist and reported seven later attempts at about 98–99% similarity; whether all seven were intentional exercise attempts has **not** been confirmed. The extra stable-start and taught-spot guard was added afterward and passed replay checks against a saved real raise plus a simulated walk. New candidate-video approval, mirrored display, and Learn/Do mode UI passed automated checks and bounded camera smoke tests, but their mouse and replay interactions still need a user live check.

The app already uses Google's [MediaPipe Pose Landmarker](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker), so moving to its GitHub repository would not change the pose system. It uses the Lite model by default. The official [Full model](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe-samples-web/blob/main/src/tasks/pose-landmarker.ts) is also downloaded locally at `models/pose_landmarker_full.task`; try it with `--model models\pose_landmarker_full.task` in either app. Full loaded and processed a 30-frame webcam smoke check here, but took longer than Lite in that short run and has **not** been compared on the same recorded full-body movement. A stronger model may improve some landmark estimates but cannot recover hidden limbs, fix a poor camera view, or by itself correct segmentation and feedback logic. Record one representative local video and run both models on the same frames before changing the default.

To validate with your movement, stand fully in frame side-on and try three normal squats, one deliberately shallow squat, and one exaggerated forward lean. Watch that knee angle approaches 180° upright and decreases during descent; a completed normal rep should count once. Then cover a leg or walk partly out of frame and verify the angle and form feedback disappear. A recorded side-view clip can be rerun with `--video` to reproduce a result. If the live view behaves differently, use `--trace` to identify the rejected landmark and angle trajectory before tuning `config.json`.

The likely later ML improvement is a validated temporal movement/form classifier trained on consented, labeled side-view exercises and bad-visibility examples. That could replace hand-tuned phase and form thresholds, while the local pose model and confidence abstention still provide the basic measurement pipeline.

The MediaPipe Lite task model is stored locally at `models/pose_landmarker_lite.task`. Its upstream location and checksum are recorded in `models/README.md`. The app does not download it at runtime.
